Challenge

A P2P lending platform for skilled freelancers had four nearly empty social channels and no dedicated social staff. The hard part wasn't volume. It was making lending and risk control, topics that feel distant, interesting enough that hairstylists, beauticians and creators, the real target users, would watch to the end.

What I did

  • YouTube: helped launch the channel from scratch and handled every step myself: thumbnails, research, shooting, scripting and editing.
  • Instagram: planned and produced content independently and created the “Meet the Pros” series with two videos a week, producing 100+ short videos in total.
  • Dcard: wrote articles and made videos that got the channel officially verified as a “promising” account, reaching the traffic threshold for official sponsored content.
  • Facebook: handled posts and short videos, and tested Meta keyword ads to attract more targeted traffic.
  • Produced three original pieces a week on fintech, new retail and AI data news, distilling the key points into everyday language.
  • Took part in data-validation model development and consumer financial behavior analysis, learning how new financial interfaces reshape decisions.

Takeaway

Running four platforms alone forced me to decide what to do and what to skip. Each audience is different: Dcard wants story, IG wants visuals, YouTube wants density, Facebook wants talking points. The same news had to be rewritten four times. That habit paid off directly when I later ran multi-platform content at Amazon.
