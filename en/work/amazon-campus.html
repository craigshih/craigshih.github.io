From concept to launch

Challenge

Amazon wanted to find cross-border e-commerce talent on Taiwanese campuses, but most students thought of “cross-border e-commerce” as little more than overseas personal shopping. We had six weeks to take a campus event from idea to launch, one that would actually convert, and first we had to convince the university's career center and individual departments to partner with us.

What I did

  • Formed the core execution team with six other T-Ambassadors and owned end-to-end planning and delivery.
  • Met one by one with the career center and 12 academic departments, opening up promotion through campus communities, official channels, department notices and student clubs.
  • Built the CEPT certification curriculum and real Amazon seller scenarios into the event, so students left with verifiable skills rather than just a talk.
  • Tapped my NCCU alumni network to recruit speakers, and designed a two-part format: a campus lecture plus one-on-one mentoring with industry advisors.
  • In the same period, led on-site execution and social content for the 9/24 Seller Accelerator Pitch Day and the 10/29 Cross-Border E-Commerce Summit.

Takeaway

The hardest part of a campus event isn't running it. It's getting people to want to come. We chose to focus on conversion: rather than chasing headcount, make sure the people who show up are willing to take the next step. The 40% registration rate came from that call.
